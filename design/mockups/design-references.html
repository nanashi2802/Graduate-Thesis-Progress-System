Which system should the Thesis Progress app borrow from?

Seven candidates, judged against what this app must do. Each one shows the real component, what it would shape in our screens, and where it falls short. Brand, color and type stay ours in every option.

Proposed: our own principle set, "Guided Ledger"

New in v2

We don't borrow one system whole. We write our own 12 principles and take each one from where it is proven: the government process systems, the modern product tools, the Refactoring UI skills in the repository, and the ui-ux-pro-max skill. The table names the source of each one, so every principle can be checked.

1. One job for each screenEach screen opens with the Docket: where am I, what blocks me, what can I do now. One primary action. GOV.UK "one thing per page" · establish-visual-hierarchy
2. Show only what is relevantYour degree level, your current milestone, your role. Nothing else. Linear "My issues" · P11
3. Always show the whole pathA compact stepper on every record screen. The full path lives on the Thesis process screen. GOV.UK step by step · USWDS step indicator
4. Empty regions teachSay what will appear, when, and the first step. Use one of four types: blank slate, informational, educational, guide. Vercel Geist empty-state framework · design-empty-states
5. Details on demandEach list row shows at most 3 facts. More opens on expand. Primer progressive disclosure · ui-ux-pro-max "overwhelm upfront"
6. Space first, rules second, cards lastGroup with spacing. Separate with a hairline. At most 3 cards on a screen. eliminate-visual-clutter · group-related-elements
7. Two densitiesComfortable for Students and Advisers. Compact for Coordinator tables, with filters and keyboard use. Linear · IBM Carbon data table
8. Maroon is action, gold is earnedMaroon only on the one thing you can do. Gold only on what is achieved. DESIGN.md §1.1
9. Paper is the recordForms show as stamps with the TDW number. The UI says "recorded" and "verified", never "signed". BR-01 · ui-ux-pro-max "document workflow" profile
10. Every blocked action explainsA disabled action shows its reason next to it. BR-18 · Stripe Apps "communicating state"
11. Plain wordsSTE at about 80%. No enum names, codes or jargon in the UI. GOV.UK content design · AGENTS.md §5
12. Accessible by defaultWCAG 2.2 AA, 44 px targets on tablets, a visible focus ring, reduced motion. ui-ux-pro-max priority 1 and 2 · better-accessibility

What ui-ux-pro-max recommends

Skill output, checked

I ran the skill's design-system search for our product type, with generic terms only and no project data. Its closest product profile is Document Workflow: "Accessible & Ethical + Minimalism & Swiss Style", a "Document Pipeline Dashboard", and a palette of trust color, green for done, amber for pending and neutral grey. That matches our tones almost exactly.

We take
  • Swiss Modernism 2.0: a strict grid, rational spacing, clear hierarchy
  • Its 141 UX rules as a review checklist: empty states, progressive disclosure, focus, touch targets
  • Its pre-delivery checklist on every screen
We reject
  • Its palette (navy and blue) and fonts (EB Garamond): our brand is fixed by the owner
  • Its page pattern ("Portfolio Grid"), which is for landing pages, not apps

GOV.UK Design System

Strongest fit for the processUK Government

Built for citizens who move through a long process run by an office, with paper and proof behind it. That is the Student's situation. Its patterns were tested with millions of users who have no training, and its writing rules match our STE rule.

Fits
  • Process screen, documents and first-day states, almost one to one
  • Strict on one thing per page and plain words
  • Rules on empty states and "what happens next"
Falls short
  • Few patterns for dense staff tables
  • No review or comment patterns
  • Plain look; our brand must carry the warmth

Open Journal Systems (PKP)

Domain analogAcademic publishing

Not a design system: a product. It runs a manuscript through Submission, Review, Copyediting and Production, with rounds of revision and a discussion in each stage. It is the closest real system to our review flow.

Fits
  • Stage tabs inside one submission
  • Filters by stage and by overdue
  • Review rounds and revisions
Falls short
  • Dated, busy interface: a model of the flow, not of the look

OJS editorial workflow guide

Instructure UI (Canvas LMS)

Familiar to students

The system behind Canvas, which many students use every week. Its learner dashboard shows what is due and what comes next.

Fits
  • Students know the look
  • "To do" and "due soon" lists
Falls short
  • Built around courses and grades; we show no grades (BR-15)
  • Weak on long, gated processes

Two ways to combine them

  1. One backbone, two specialists. GOV.UK sets the principles and every Student-facing pattern. Carbon shapes the Coordinator tables. Primer shapes the manuscript workspace. Each source owns named screens, so they never mix on one page.
  2. One source only. GOV.UK for everything, and we design our own table and review patterns. Simpler to explain; more design work for the Coordinator and the workspace.
